B2: Masking AMOUNTs in states that report them well. Results (run 2026-10-30 20:30 PT)
The pre-registration is in b2_mask_amounts_prereg.txt, written at 20:25 before any B2 result. It is not changed here.

Deviation from the plan, disclosed. The first run (20:26) had a coding bug in F2: the training class means were
keyed by "state-year.class" instead of "class", so every lookup missed, F2 received all-zero AMOUNTs, and it
reproduced F0 exactly. I fixed the lookup (one line, unname(which(tr))) and reran at 20:30. The design, seed,
folds, metrics and criteria are unchanged. F0 and F1 numbers are identical across the two runs. The first run's
log and results are kept as scratch/b2_mask_amounts_run1_f2bug.log and scratch/b2_results_run1_f2bug.rds.

1. Sample
- 22 state-years meet the report-well rule: Illinois 2022; Kansas 2024; Montana 2017-19 and 2022-24;
  New Hampshire 2024; North Dakota 2022-24; Virginia 2017-19 and 2022-24; West Virginia 2017-19 and 2024.
- They hold 517 clean_split cases, of which 413 take the capped path and reach RAWBEN unmasked. These 413
  are the evaluation cases. 339 have two elements, 65 three, 9 four.
- The informative "other" cases (41 nationally) count toward the report-well share only, as planned.
- Sensitivity set: every clean_split case nationally, 820, of which 645 (86 state-years) are capped and reach.

3. Breakdowns, report-well (all-agree rate, F0 zero arm unless noted)
- By number of elements: two 39.8% (n 339); three 15.4% (n 65); four 0% (n 9).
- By true slot-1 share (the share of the gap that slot 1's variable produces in the AMOUNT-guided result):
  at least 0.9: 45.2% (n 186); under 0.9: 26.9% (n 227). The median true slot-1 share is 0.83. The
  interquartile range runs from 0.28 to 1.04, and 10% of cases are below -0.12. In a typical masked case,
  then, the greedy rule gives slot 1 the whole gap when the true split is shared.
- By element mix (n >= 15): rent|util 63.6%; earn|unearn 51.9%; unearn|earn 43.8%; earn|rent 41.9%;
  unearn|rent 38.0%; rent|unearn 36.0%; rent|med 26.7%. F2 helps rent|med (40.0%) and hurts unearn|earn
  (6.2%) and rent|unearn (12.0%).
- By state: Virginia 39.8%; Montana 38.1%; West Virginia 37.6%; New Hampshire 33.3%; Illinois 31.2%;
  Kansas 30.0%; North Dakota 20.0%. Every state is below 50%.
- By household size: 1, 40.9%; 2, 30.0%; 3, 30.8%; 4+, 30.4%.

4. Sensitivity, all clean_split cases (n = 645, 86 clusters)
- F0 zero 35.5% [32.1, 38.9], MAE $159; copied 33.5%; amount1_only = zero; F1 18.9%; F2 31.8%.
- F1 - F0: -16.6 points [-20.7, -12.2]; MAE +$31 [+12, +49].
  F2 - F0: -3.7 points [-5.9, -1.5]; MAE +$24 [+5, +44].
- By true slot-1 share: at least 0.9, 50.9%; under 0.9, 23.2%. Under 0.9, F1 (24.0%) roughly equals F0.
- Small states vary widely (North Carolina 100% of 10; Oregon 52.9% of 17; Alabama 0% of 6). The national

5. Why the exploratory figure was higher
The exploratory run counted 47.3% agreement on all 820 clean_split cases. B2 keeps only the cases where the
unmasked run takes the capped path and reaches RAWBEN, so the truth is well defined. The excluded cases
include household-size and homeless shifts and cases where the capped path itself stops short. A likely
reason, not measured, is that masked and unmasked runs of those cases often stop at the same limit and agree
for that reason. The pre-registered figure, 35%, is the one to use.

6. Conclusion
- Grade. By the pre-registered rule, the greedy fallback is poor: 35.1% of masked cases [30.9, 39.3]
  recover every variable within $10 or 2%, well under the 50% line. The result does not depend on how the
  AMOUNTs are hidden (zeros 35.1%, AMTERR copied 33.2%, AMTERR in slot 1 35.1%). It holds in every
  report-well state and in the national sensitivity set.
- Reaching RAWBEN does not validate the split. 93-96% of masked cases still reach RAWBEN exactly. The
  greedy rule finds a combination of values that produces the issued benefit, but in about two thirds of
  cases it is not the combination QC's AMOUNTs imply. Agreement on how much of the gap slot 1 carries
  (within 0.10) is 36%.
- What can be trusted. On the 83% of multi-element error cases with all_zero, copied or amount1_only
  AMOUNTs:
  - Case-level outputs that depend only on reaching RAWBEN are sound.
  - Single-element cases are not affected by this test.
  - For multi-element cases, the variable-level values are a plausible split, not a measured one. They are
    right about four times in ten for two elements and about one time in seven for three or more.
  - The greedy rule does best when one element truly carries the error (45-51% when slot 1 carries 90% or
    more). It does worst when the error is shared.
- Which fallback. Neither alternative beats F0, so F0 stays, as the plan specified.
  - The proportional split (F1) is much worse: 16.5 points lower, with higher MAE. Average shares put
    every case at a middle split, but the true splits are spread out, from slot 1 carrying almost nothing
    to slot 1 carrying more than the whole gap.
  - Ordering by each variable's typical size (F2) is slightly but reliably worse: 2.9 points lower, MAE
    $27 higher. QC's slot order carries more information about which element is largest than the
    variable type does.
- What this implies for the munging:
  (a) Keep the greedy rule. It is the best of the three tested rules.
  (b) Flag uncapped multi-element error cases (cascade_type "uncapped", two or more Step 7 elements)
      as having an imputed variable split, so analyses can see them.
  (c) Anything that uses an individual reconstructed variable on error cases should be rerun without
      these cases, or with them down-weighted. That covers variable-level rules, the share of error
      dollars attributed to earned income versus shelter, and models trained on reconstructed raw
      values. Benefit-level labels and dollars do not need this.
  (d) Do not cite the reconstructed variable split for multi-element cases as data. If a better split
      is wanted, the next candidate is a model of the slot-1 share conditional on the element mix, natures
      and the gap size, judged on this same masked set. Because the true shares are spread out, a gain
      is not assured.
- Limitations.
  - The report-well states (WV, MT, VA, ND, IL, KS, NH) are a handful. Their error mix and the way their
    reviewers split errors may differ from states that report zeros. The national clean_split sensitivity
    run gives the same answer, but every state in it also writes clean splits, so the limitation stands.
  - The truth is itself a reconstruction. It assumes QC's AMOUNTs are correct and that the code's order
    (utilities, then largest AMOUNT) matches the order in which QC computed them. If QC's AMOUNTs are
    themselves rough, 35% is a measure of agreement with QC, not of accuracy against the real raw values,
    and could be off in either direction. It is still
    the only truth available in the public file.
  - Agreement uses a $10-or-2% tolerance. Looser tolerances would raise all rates. They probably would not
    change the ordering of the methods, since the MAE differences point the same way (not run).
