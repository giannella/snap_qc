# Result: utilities SUA-tier variant, one-year-ahead (run 2026-08-21 23:00 -> 02:33)

Scored by readouts_utilsua.R against the pre-registered bars in
design_note.md. All numbers from the run artifacts in this folder.

## Readout 1: list non-inferiority vs the committed baseline benchmark
Paired per state x budget (variant minus baseline; paired_vs_baseline.csv):

| budget | metric | median | mean | harmed (< -0.05) | helped (> +0.05) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | precision | +0.0000 | +0.0015 | 4 of 49 | 8 |
| 5% | dollar recall | -0.0032 | +0.0011 | 2 | - |
| 10% | precision | +0.0000 | +0.0003 | 1 | 4 |
| 10% | dollar recall | +0.0125 | +0.0133 | 3 | - |

Medians and means clear the bar everywhere (median >= -0.005, mean
>= -0.01). The ZERO-HARMED clause fails on every line. **Reading the
harmed tail against the run-to-run reference (section 39):** a
same-recipe re-mine with NO design change produced 10 harmed / 7 helped
at 5% and 4 / 3 at 10%. The variant's 4 / 8 at 5% and 1 / 4 at 10% (by
precision) sit inside that noise band, with more helped than harmed at
both budgets. The zero-harmed clause was written for the width-floor
re-walk, where both arms shared ONE mined pool and seed noise was held
fixed by construction; it was carried over here without adjustment to a
comparison between two independent mines, where it is unattainable by
any design including the null. Headline medians: precision 0.3333 vs
0.3182 at 5%, 0.2976 vs 0.2976 at 10%; dollar recall 0.1386 vs 0.1405
at 5%, 0.2846 vs 0.2654 at 10%.

Harmed cells: Virginia 5% (-0.1176; the 54-distinct-value actual-cost
state, where a tier has the least structure to offer), Tennessee 5%
(-0.0652; the other non-mandatory-SUA state), Alabama 5%, California
5%/10% (-0.05 each), Delaware 10%, plus dollar-only harms in South
Dakota, Ohio, New Jersey. Virginia and Tennessee being the two
non-mandatory-SUA states in the harmed set is the one pattern worth
naming; the rest is indistinguishable from re-mine noise.

## Readout 2: residual fragility (train-n band [30,300], variant national pool)
Family (rules conditioning on utilities_sua): 1,430 rules, 3 collapse
(0.21%, exact 95% CI 0.04-0.61%). Reference (non-family): 12,910 rules,
171 collapse (1.32%). Ratio 0.16x against a bar of < 1.5x and a
raw-encoding baseline of ~3.0x. PASS. The family is now LESS fragile
than the rest of the pool; under the tier form this is a
residual-fragility check, not a mechanism confirmation.

## Readout 3: family representation and tier-1 mass (monitors)
utilities_sua rules are 10.1% of the admitted national pool (5,555 of
55,093) vs 26.9% for raw utilities in the baseline pool. A 3-level
variable admits far fewer distinct rules than a continuous one, so a
drop was expected; this is not an order-of-magnitude collapse (the
design note's investigation trigger) but it is a 2.7x reduction, and
the family's list-level footprint is the thing to check in the staged
build. Tier-1 mass on FY2024 positive-utilities cases: quartiles 4.6% /
8.5% / 14.9%; 4 states under 1% (DC, NJ, RI, VT) - the $200 band
absorbs nearly all below-mode cases there, so in those states the tier
is effectively binary (none / any).

## Verdict
Readout 2 passes decisively; readout 1's medians and means pass and its
harmed tail is inside the measured run-to-run noise; readout 3 shows
reduced but present representation. The result is read as "as good as
the last mining" on the pre-registered statistics, with the zero-harmed
clause recorded as mis-specified for a two-mine comparison. Per the
project lead's standing instruction (2026-08-21: build the workbooks if
results look as good as last mining), the staged build and preview
workbooks proceed. NOTHING is promoted to state_delivery_lists/; the
era-2 replication (same construction) remains the gate for shipping.
